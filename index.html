Atlas · interface explorations

One assistant.
Five interfaces.

Atlas is an always-on voice assistant living on a Raspberry Pi with a 10.1″ touchscreen in a matte-black frame. These are five complete, rival designs for everything it shows — thirty-one screens each, from the idle clock to deep research with source tabs, live-ticking timers, a two-phase calendar confirm, and a camera viewfinder. Every one is fully clickable, and every ground is pure black, so the glass disappears into the frame. Best viewed landscape; built for 1280 × 800 touch.

01 Mark VII OPEN → A holographic cockpit HUD on absolute void. One rule everywhere: every ring encodes a real quantity — the clock's arc is progress through the day, timers are targeting rings that deplete, and touch targets snap amber lock-on brackets. 02 Velvet Observatory OPEN → Planetarium-grade minimalism: jet black, one ice-blue voice, and an enormous serif clock whose seconds hand is a small body on an ellipse. The navigator is an orrery — every screen is a moon you tap. 03 Phosphor Deck OPEN → A glass cockpit with CRT-phosphor soul. Timers are drum counters, the waveform is an oscilloscope, and anything destructive hides under a hazard-striped switch guard you physically lift before you can throw it. 04 Biolume OPEN → The assistant as a deep-sea organism: a living particle medusa that breathes at idle, reaches into your voice when listening, coils while thinking, and pulses as it speaks. No rectangle in the design has four equal corners. 05 Redline OPEN → Kinetic typographic mission control — type is the interface. The clock is a giant split-flap board, state changes flip like departures, and red is rationed to what is live, armed, or destructive.

Each demo is one self-contained file: no frameworks, no network requests, all data synthetic. Timers genuinely tick, tabs switch, sound previews synthesize live, and confirm flows advance — tap the corner index inside any demo to jump between all thirty-one screens.

Atlas itself runs on-device: wake word, realtime voice, camera, calendar, mail, and a grounding policy that would rather say "I don't know" than guess.
