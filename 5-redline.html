Restaurant Technology News · 22 July 2026 Square vs Toast for very small food businesses
read % 7 min

The short version

For a business doing under fifty transactions a day, the deciding number is not the processing rate — every major processor sits within a quarter point of the others. It is the monthly software floor, and whether the terminal keeps taking payments when the network drops.

Square charges nothing per month until you want the extras. Toast charges from the first day, and its cheapest plan still assumes a kitchen.

What the fees actually are

Square takes 2.6 % + 10¢ on a tapped or dipped card, with no monthly software fee on the free plan. Toast takes 2.49 % + 15¢ on its own hardware but attaches a software subscription that starts at $69 a month once you add anything beyond a single terminal.

On a forty-order Sunday averaging $9.40 a ticket, the rate difference is worth about eleven cents. The subscription is worth sixty-nine dollars. The rate is a distraction.

Offline mode is the real divider

Square’s offline mode stores card presentations locally and settles them when the connection returns, capped at a total dollar amount you set. Toast’s offline behaviour depends on its own router being alive; if you are running off a phone hotspot at a farmers’ market, that is a different risk profile entirely.

For a stall at Tower Grove with no fixed line, the ability to keep selling through a dead cell patch is worth more than any rate.

Hardware you can actually carry

A Square Reader is $59 and fits in an apron pocket. A Toast Go 2 is $609 and is built for table service. The Clover Flex sits between them at $499 but locks you to a merchant account you cannot easily leave.

Where this leaves a creamery

A single-counter, single-operator dessert business with a weekend market stall wants zero monthly floor, pocketable hardware, and offline tolerance. That is one product.
